Is $1,250 every two weeks good?

$1,250 every two weeks is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $2,354 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 68% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $304 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: cashiers ($15.81/hr), fast food and counter workers ($15.00/hr), bartenders ($16.51/hr), childcare workers ($16.82/hr).

How it compares

$15.62 an hour is 2.2× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 50% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
