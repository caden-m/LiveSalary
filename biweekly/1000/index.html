Is $1,000 every two weeks good?

$1,000 every two weeks is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that MIT's Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult with no children needs on average in the US.

In practice it means about $1,909 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 84% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and groceries there is nothing left at national prices; the numbers only work with lower housing costs or additional income.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: fast food and counter workers ($14.20/hr).

How it compares

$12.50 an hour is 1.7× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 58% below the median full-time US wage of about $29.90 an hour ($62,192 a year).
