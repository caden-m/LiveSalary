Is $500 every two weeks good?

$500 every two weeks is below the federal minimum wage, so it's only legal in limited cases such as tipped work, some students and trainees. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $1,000 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent is more than the whole month's take-home, so this wage alone can't cover a one-bedroom at national prices. After rent and groceries there is nothing left at national prices; the numbers only work with lower housing costs or additional income.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: fast food and counter workers ($15.00/hr), cashiers ($15.81/hr), bartenders ($16.51/hr).

How it compares

$6.25 an hour is 0.9× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 80% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
