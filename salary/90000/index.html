Is $90,000 a year good?

$90,000 a year is comfortably above the US median — typical of skilled trades and experienced professionals. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that MIT's Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult with no children needs on average in the US.

In practice it means about $5,975 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 27% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $3,925 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: registered nurses ($45.00/hr), accountants ($39.27/hr), mechanical engineers ($49.19/hr), police officers ($37.15/hr).

How it compares

$43.27 an hour is 6.0× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 45% above the median full-time US wage of about $29.90 an hour ($62,192 a year).
