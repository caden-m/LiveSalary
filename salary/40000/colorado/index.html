Colorado income tax on $40,000 a year

Colorado has a flat 4.40% income tax after a $15,000 standard deduction. Flat 4.4%, starting from federal taxable income, so the deduction is the federal standard deduction ($15,000 pre-OBBBA; $15,750 if the state follows the 2025 federal increase). Denver, Aurora, Glendale, Greenwood Village and Sheridan levy small monthly occupational privilege taxes (not modeled).

Compared with a state with no income tax, living in Colorado costs about $1,100 a year on this income — $92 a month. Nationally (federal taxes only), take-home would be $34,178.

How $40,000 a year compares in Colorado

$40,000 a year is 1.3× the Colorado minimum wage of $14.81 and below the national median full-time wage of $29.90 an hour. The median household income in Colorado is about $97,113 (US: $80,610), so $40,000 a year from one earner is 41% of a typical household's income here.
