Is $62,000 a year good?

$62,000 a year is right around the US median full-time wage. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $4,329 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 37% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $2,279 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: computer user support specialists ($29.74/hr), news analysts, reporters and journalists ($29.90/hr), insurance sales agents ($29.94/hr), chefs and head cooks ($30.03/hr).

How it compares

$29.81 an hour is 4.1× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 5% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
