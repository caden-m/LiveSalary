Is $39,000 a year good?

$39,000 a year is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $2,789 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 57% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $739 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: construction laborers ($22.47/hr), cashiers ($15.00/hr), fast food and counter workers ($14.65/hr).

How it compares

$18.75 an hour is 2.6× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 40% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
