Is $18,000 a month good?

$18,000 a month is far above the US median — top-percentile pay. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $13,389 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 12% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $11,339 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: chief executives ($102.88/hr), general pediatricians ($100.98/hr), airline pilots, copilots and flight engineers ($111.61/hr), family medicine physicians ($117.39/hr).

How it compares

$103.85 an hour is 14.3× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 232% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
