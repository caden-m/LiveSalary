Is $30,000 a month good?

$30,000 a month is far above the US median — top-percentile pay. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $21,032 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 8% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $18,982 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: surgeons ($177.66/hr), anesthesiologists ($188.22/hr).

How it compares

$173.08 an hour is 23.9× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 453% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
