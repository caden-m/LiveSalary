Is $4,500 a month good?

$4,500 a month is below the US median, in the range of many service, retail and early-career jobs. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that MIT's Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult with no children needs on average in the US.

In practice it means about $3,786 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 42% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $1,736 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers ($27.62/hr), firefighters ($28.77/hr), chefs and head cooks ($29.32/hr), graphic designers ($29.47/hr).

How it compares

$25.96 an hour is 3.6× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 13% below the median full-time US wage of about $29.90 an hour ($62,192 a year).
