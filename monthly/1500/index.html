Is $1,500 a month good?

$1,500 a month is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $1,366 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent is more than the whole month's take-home, so this wage alone can't cover a one-bedroom at national prices. After rent and groceries there is nothing left at national prices; the numbers only work with lower housing costs or additional income.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: fast food and counter workers ($14.65/hr), cashiers ($15.00/hr), construction laborers ($22.47/hr).

How it compares

$8.65 an hour is 1.2× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 72% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
