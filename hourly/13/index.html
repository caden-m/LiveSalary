Is $13 an hour good?

$13 an hour is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $1,987 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 81% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and groceries there is nothing left at national prices; the numbers only work with lower housing costs or additional income.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: fast food and counter workers ($14.65/hr).

How it compares

$13 an hour is 1.8× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 58% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
