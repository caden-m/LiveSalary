Is $19 an hour good?

$19 an hour is below the US median, in the range of many service, retail and early-career jobs. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $2,824 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 57% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $774 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: landscaping and groundskeeping workers ($18.82/hr), security guards ($18.28/hr), receptionists and information clerks ($18.27/hr), restaurant cooks ($17.98/hr).

How it compares

$19 an hour is 2.6× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 39% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
