Utah income tax on $67 an hour

Utah has a flat 4.50% income tax. Flat 4.5% for 2025 (cut from 4.55% by HB 106, 2025). No standard deduction: Utah gives a taxpayer credit of 6% of the federal standard deduction (about $900), phased out by 1.3% of income above roughly $18,000, so it is worth a few hundred dollars at typical incomes (not modeled).

Compared with a state with no income tax, living in Utah costs about $6,271 a year on this income — $523 a month. Nationally (federal taxes only), take-home would be $106,006.

How $67 an hour compares in Utah

$67 an hour is 9.2× the Utah minimum wage of $7.25 (the federal rate) and above the national median full-time wage of $29.90 an hour. The median household income in Utah is about $96,658 (US: $80,610), so $67 an hour from one earner is 144% of a typical household's income here.
