Is $35 an hour good?

$35 an hour is comfortably above the US median — typical of skilled trades and experienced professionals. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $4,980 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 32% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $2,930 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: high school teachers ($34.63/hr), human resources specialists ($36.51/hr), police officers ($36.64/hr), librarians and media collections specialists ($32.82/hr).

How it compares

$35 an hour is 4.8× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 12% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
