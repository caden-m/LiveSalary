Is $25 an hour good?

$25 an hour is below the US median, in the range of many service, retail and early-career jobs. It's right at the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $3,659 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 44% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $1,609 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: real estate sales agents ($25.40/hr), automotive service technicians and mechanics ($24.34/hr), welders, cutters, solderers and brazers ($25.84/hr), secretaries and administrative assistants ($22.86/hr).

How it compares

$25 an hour is 3.4× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 20% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
