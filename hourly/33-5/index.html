Is $33.50 an hour good?

$33.50 an hour is right around the US median full-time wage. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $4,798 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 33% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $2,748 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: librarians and media collections specialists ($32.82/hr), high school teachers ($34.63/hr), athletes and sports competitors ($32.07/hr), elementary school teachers ($30.75/hr).

How it compares

$33.50 an hour is 4.6× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 7% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
