Is $38 an hour good?

$38 an hour is comfortably above the US median — typical of skilled trades and experienced professionals. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $5,346 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 30% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $3,296 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: police officers ($36.64/hr), human resources specialists ($36.51/hr), accountants ($40.23/hr), high school teachers ($34.63/hr).

How it compares

$38 an hour is 5.2× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 22% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
