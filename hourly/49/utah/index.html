Utah income tax on $49 an hour

Utah has a flat 4.50% income tax. Flat 4.5% for 2025 (cut from 4.55% by HB 106, 2025). No standard deduction: Utah gives a taxpayer credit of 6% of the federal standard deduction (about $900), phased out by 1.3% of income above roughly $18,000, so it is worth a few hundred dollars at typical incomes (not modeled).

Compared with a state with no income tax, living in Utah costs about $4,586 a year on this income — $382 a month. Nationally (federal taxes only), take-home would be $80,252.

How $49 an hour compares in Utah

$49 an hour is 6.8× the Utah minimum wage of $7.25 (the federal rate) and above the national median full-time wage of $31.27 an hour. The median household income in Utah is about $96,658 (US: $81,604), so $49 an hour from one earner is 105% of a typical household's income here.
