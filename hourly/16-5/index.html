Is $16.50 an hour good?

$16.50 an hour is well below the US median — a typical entry-level or part-time wage. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that MIT's Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult with no children needs on average in the US.

In practice it means about $2,468 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 65% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $418 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: cashiers ($14.76/hr), fast food and counter workers ($14.20/hr).

How it compares

$16.50 an hour is 2.3× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 45% below the median full-time US wage of about $29.90 an hour ($62,192 a year).
