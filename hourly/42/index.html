Is $42 an hour good?

$42 an hour is comfortably above the US median — typical of skilled trades and experienced professionals. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $5,834 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 27% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $3,784 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: accountants ($39.27/hr), registered nurses ($45.00/hr), police officers ($36.68/hr).

How it compares

$42 an hour is 5.8× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 34% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
