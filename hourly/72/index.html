Is $72 an hour good?

$72 an hour is well above the US median, in the range of senior professionals and many managers. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $9,441 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 17% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $7,391 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: air traffic controllers ($71.19/hr), pharmacists ($67.75/hr), lawyers ($76.76/hr), software developers ($65.38/hr).

How it compares

$72 an hour is 9.9× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 130% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
