Is $125 an hour good?

$125 an hour is far above the US median — top-percentile pay. It's above the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $15,796 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 10% of take-home, under the 30% guideline, leaving room to save. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $13,746 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: family medicine physicians ($114.61/hr), airline pilots, copilots and flight engineers ($108.94/hr).

How it compares

$125 an hour is 17.2× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 300% above the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
