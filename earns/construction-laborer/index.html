A construction laborer earns about $22.14 an hour

The median annual wage for construction laborers in the US is about $46,050 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, May 2024 (approximate medians)). Over a standard 40-hour week that works out to $22.14 an hour, $3,838 a month, or $177 a day.

Is $46,050 a year good?

$46,050 a year is below the US median, in the range of many service, retail and early-career jobs. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that MIT's Living Wage Calculator estimates a single adult with no children needs on average in the US.

In practice it means about $3,253 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 49% of take-home — above the 30% guideline, workable with a careful budget. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $1,203 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers ($27.62/hr), firefighters ($28.77/hr), chefs and head cooks ($29.32/hr).

How it compares

$22.14 an hour is 3.1× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 26% below the median full-time US wage of about $29.90 an hour ($62,192 a year).
