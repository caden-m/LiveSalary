A emt earns about $21.38 an hour

The median annual wage for emergency medical technicians in the US is about $44,470 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, OEWS May 2025 (national medians)). Over a standard 40-hour week that works out to $21.38 an hour, $3,706 a month, or $171 a day.

Is $44,470 a year good?

$44,470 a year is below the US median, in the range of many service, retail and early-career jobs. It's below the roughly $25 an hour that a single adult with no children typically needs to cover basic costs in the US, going by MIT's Living Wage Calculator state estimates (which run from the low $20s to over $30 in expensive states).

In practice it means about $3,155 a month after federal taxes. At that level a typical $1,600 rent would take about 51% of take-home — far above the 30% most budgets aim for, so roommates, a cheaper area or a second income are usually part of the picture. After rent and about $450 of groceries, roughly $1,105 a month is left for everything else: transport, insurance, phone, debt, savings.

Jobs whose median pay is close to this: customer service representatives ($21.52/hr), light truck drivers ($21.57/hr), medical assistants ($21.97/hr), construction laborers ($22.65/hr).

How it compares

$21.38 an hour is 2.9× the federal minimum wage of $7.25 and 32% below the median full-time US wage of about $31.27 an hour ($65,052 a year).
