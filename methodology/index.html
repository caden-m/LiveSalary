Methodology

Every figure on this site is calculated, not copied. This page lists the assumptions and data behind them so you can judge how well they fit your situation.

Working time

Annual pay from an hourly rate assumes 40 hours a week for 52 weeks = 2,080 hours a year, with no unpaid time off. Monthly figures divide the year by 12; biweekly by 26; weekly by 52; daily by 260 working days. Each page also shows the result for 20–50 hour weeks.

Federal taxes (2025, single filer)

Take-home estimates subtract three things from gross pay:

  • Federal income tax using the 2025 brackets for a single filer after the $15,750 standard deduction (the amount set by the July 2025 tax law, up from the $15,000 announced earlier): 10% to $11,925, 12% to $48,475, 22% to $103,350, 24% to $197,300, 32% to $250,525, 35% to $626,350, then 37%.
  • Social Security at 6.2% of wages up to $176,100.
  • Medicare at 1.45%, plus 0.9% on wages over $200,000.

Not included: tax credits, itemized deductions, 401(k) or HSA contributions, health insurance premiums, filing jointly, dependants. Real take-home is usually a little higher for people with pre-tax benefits and credits, and lower for people with local taxes.

State taxes

State pages add state income tax for tax year 2025 using each state's single-filer brackets and standard deduction (or the closest equivalent where a state uses exemptions or credits instead). Local and city income taxes are not included; pages for states where they are common say so. Source: state revenue departments and the Tax Foundation's annual state tax survey; compiled 2026-10-17. Treat these as estimates good to a few hundred dollars a year, not a filing calculation.

Comparisons

  • Federal minimum wage: $7.25 an hour (unchanged since 2009). State pages use the state minimum in effect in 2025.
  • US median wage: $1,251 a week for full-time wage and salary workers (BLS, median usual weekly earnings, Q2 2026, not seasonally adjusted), treated as $65,052 a year or $31.27 an hour.
  • Living wage: about $25 an hour for a single adult with no children. MIT's Living Wage Calculator publishes county and state estimates, not a national one; its 2026 state figures for one adult range from roughly the low $20s to over $30 (California $30.48, Washington $26.59), so $25 is used here as a rough national mid-point.
  • Median household income: $81,604 nationally (Census ACS 2024 1-year estimate); state figures on state pages are from the same release. Household income usually combines more than one earner, so it is not directly comparable with one person's pay.
  • Budget illustrations: $1,600 rent (roughly the national median asking rent) and $450 a month of groceries for one adult. Your costs will differ; the point is scale, not precision.

Occupation pay

Median annual wages for occupations are the national medians from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2024 release, checked against the BLS data file. "Median" means half of workers in that job earn more and half earn less; pay varies widely by state, employer and experience. Where the site uses a plain job name for a BLS category (for example "family doctor" for family medicine physicians, or "dentist" for all dentists), the broader figure is used.

Celebrity and executive earnings

Athletes and musicians use Forbes' estimates of 12-month earnings (salary, prize money, endorsements and other income, before tax and fees) from its 2025 highest-paid lists. Executives use total compensation reported in company proxy statements, which is mostly stock awards whose eventual value differs. These are public estimates, not audited figures, and are refreshed when new lists are published. Per-second figures divide the annual estimate evenly across all 31,536,000 seconds of the year.

The live counter

The counter multiplies the chosen rate by the exact time elapsed, so it stays correct even if your device slows down or the tab is hidden. It never rounds up: the large digits show whole cents, the small digits show the next two decimal places.

Updates and corrections

Assumptions are reviewed when new tax brackets, BLS wage data or Forbes lists are published, normally once a year. Pages show their last update date in the footer. Found an error? Let me know.
