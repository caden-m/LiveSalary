About Live Salary

Live Salary started in 2024 as a one-page toy: type your hourly rate, press Start, and watch the money count up. It turns out watching your pay arrive second by second changes how an hour feels — and makes abstract numbers like "$25 an hour" or "$275 million a year" suddenly concrete.

What's here

The live counter is the heart of the site. Around it are pages that answer the questions people actually type into a search box: what an hourly wage adds up to over a year, what a salary works out to per hour, what's left after taxes in each state, and how fast the highest-paid people on earth earn compared with everyday jobs. Every page has the counter preloaded with that rate.

Who makes it

Live Salary is made and maintained by Caden, an independent developer in the United States, with help from AI tools for code and research. There's no company behind it. The code is open at GitHub.

How it's funded

Right now it isn't. The site may carry a small number of ads on the reference pages in future; the counter itself will stay ad-free. There are no sponsored figures, affiliate placements or paid rankings on any page.

Accuracy

Every number on the site is computed from stated assumptions and public data; the methodology page lists them with sources and dates. If something looks wrong, say so — corrections ship within days.
